# The Architecture of Fourth-Dimensional Consciousness & The Law of Assumption: Neville Goddard's *Out of this World* (1949)

## Executive Architectural Summary

In his 1949 masterwork ***Out of this World***, Neville Goddard advances his foundational philosophy beyond psychological introspection into the realm of **relativistic spatiotemporal geometry**. Written five years after *Feeling Is the Secret* (1944), Goddard reconciles the age-old philosophical contradiction between **strict predetermination** and **human free will**.

The core premise of *Out of this World* is that the universe is a **dimensionally continuous manifold**. What human beings perceive as "time" is merely the linear, sequential bisection of a pre-existing fourth-dimensional reality. Every event, encounter, object, and condition already exists fully formed in a dimensionally larger space. Just as a solid bisected yields a two-dimensional surface, the three-dimensional physical universe is simply a rolling cross section of a four-dimensional continuum slowly moving through our sensory field.

Man does not generate, construct, or manufacture his future through physical labor; he merely travels through coordinates that are already actualized. Determinism governs the three-dimensional observer confined to the sensory "now," but **plasticity governs the four-dimensional operator**. By mastering the movements of his attention, entering the hypnagogic state of relaxed immobility, and cultivating **attention minus effort**, the operator consciously alters his **concept of self**. This change in self-definition instantly switches the track of his trajectory across the multidimensional block universe, producing a deterministic sequence of physical events that harden into objective fact.

## Chapter 1: Thinking Fourth-Dimensionally

### 1. The Premonition Paradox and the Geometry of Time
Goddard opens his treatise with an empirical observation that challenges the classical Newtonian model of linear causality: **precognition**. Many individuals observe events before they occur in the three dimensions of space. From a strict philosophical perspective, if an event can be observed before it happens on earth, it must already exist somewhere:
> *"Since man can observe an event before it occurs in the three dimensions of space, life on earth must proceed according to plan, and this plan must exist elsewhere in another dimension and be slowly moving through our space."* (John 14:29)

If an event is observed before its arrival in the three-dimensional world, it is, by definition, **"out of this world."** To the ordinary waking observer, this appears as unyielding predetermination. If the future can be seen, it must already be cast in concrete. This leads to the fundamental question: *Are we able to alter our future?*

Goddard's answer forms the thesis of fourth-dimensional metacognition:
* **The future is prepared in every detail in advance**, possessing several branching potential outcomes.
* Man alters his future not through external physical interference, but through an internal shift in consciousness.
* Once altered at the coordinate of consciousness, the future forms a new, completely deterministic sequence starting from that point of interference.
* The ultimate cornerstone of reality is **man's concept of himself**. Man acts, lives, and experiences purely as an outward manifestation of his inner self-definition.

### 2. The Carnal Focus vs. The Spiritual Focus
Every human being possesses two distinct perceptive faculties:
1. **The Natural Focus ("The Carnal Mind")**: Governed by the five physical senses and constrained to the fleeting slice of time called "now." To the natural focus, past and future are ephemeral abstractions, and reality is limited strictly to physical surfaces.
2. **The Spiritual Focus ("The Mind of Christ")**: Governed by controlled imagination and driven by deep desire. It perceives the contents of time as an extended landscape. Events separated by years or miles are observed as distinct objects co-existing in space.

```

```

### 3. Dimensional Analogy: The Cross Section Principle
To make the fourth dimension comprehensible to three-dimensional intellects, Goddard utilizes the geometrical principle of dimensional reduction:
* Bisect a **solid** (3D) $\rightarrow$ You produce a **surface** (2D).
* Bisect a **surface** (2D) $\rightarrow$ You produce a **line** (1D).
* Bisect a **line** (1D) $\rightarrow$ You produce a **point** (0D).

Following this inexorable mathematical logic:
$$\text{Point (0D)} \subset \text{Line (1D)} \subset \text{Surface (2D)} \subset \text{Solid (3D)} \subset \text{Hyper-body (4D)}$$

A three-dimensional human being is not an isolated, instantaneous entity. When you look at an individual, you are observing only a momentary **cross section** of their complete four-dimensional self. The full four-dimensional human is an elongated spatiotemporal continuum containing every moment, experience, breath, and action from birth to death, all co-existing simultaneously as a living, animated whole.

Time is simply the measurement of this four-dimensional dimension. It is a medium for changes in experience. Because all three-dimensional objects are cross sections of four-dimensional realities, **any change in the 3D world must originate from a reorientation within the 4D manifold**.

### 4. The Serial Universe of J.W. Dunne
Goddard integrates the theoretical frameworks of British aeronautical engineer J.W. Dunne (*An Experiment with Time*, *The Serial Universe*):
* Space and time are serial. Our three-dimensional world is an abstraction from a dimensionally larger fourth-dimensional world.
* That fourth-dimensional world is itself an abstraction from a fifth-dimensional world, regressing toward infinity.
* In practical human application, man does not need to master infinite dimensions; he only needs to operate between two:
  1. **The world known through bodily organs** (the 3D physical plane).
  2. **The world perceived independently of bodily organs** (the 4D imaginal plane).

## Chapter 2: Assumptions Become Facts

### 1. Removing the Veil of the Senses
Men revere the external world as solid, immutable fact because they do not know how to focus their mental energies to penetrate its thin crust. To pierce this sensory veil, brute physical exertion is useless. The physical world dissolves not through aggressive resistance, but through **attention withdrawal**:
* Turn the attention entirely away from sensory evidence.
* Concentrate upon the invisible state until it acquires the full distinctness and somatic density of objective reality.
* By granting the imaginal state sensory vividness, you bestow upon it the legal right to become a visible concrete fact.

### 2. The Internal Dialogue as an Accurate Self-Concept Mirror
How can an operator verify whether an assumption has actually taken root in consciousness? Goddard introduces a diagnostic test: **audit your internal conversations**.
In mental dialogues, humans are completely uninhibited and brutally honest. If, in your imagination, you interact with friends, employers, or rivals from the same position of insecurity, limitation, or complaint as before, you have not changed your concept of self. 

When your concept of self changes, your relationship to the entire world changes. In meditation, you must see others looking at you and interacting with you exactly as they would if your desired state were already an accomplished physical fact.

```

       - Explains why things failed.                   - Assumes quiet, normal ownership.
       - Pleads for external validation.               - Acts from effortless completion.
       - Looks AT the desired state from afar.         - Looks FROM the desired state into life.
       =================================================================
```

### 3. Neutralizing Longing: Believing and Being Are One
Desire is the psychological awareness of a lack. The moment an assumption is truly accepted as reality within the fourth-dimensional mind, **all longing is neutralized**. 
* You cannot continue yearning for what you already possess.
* Fulfillment dissolves the friction of desire.
* Believing and being are identical: the conceiver and his conception are one.
* Nearness implies distance; therefore, your ideal must not be viewed as "near" or "approaching"—it must be embodied as an immediate, present state of being: *"I AM."*

### 4. The Page Rearrangement Model & Dream Cybernetics
To resolve how the future can be both predetermined and subject to human intervention, Goddard introduces the **Printed Page Analogy**:
* Imagine every word on a printed page represents a single sensory impression.
* When reading normally, you scan words singly and sequentially from left to right, deriving one specific narrative.
* All words on the page are equally present at the exact same time.
* If you detach the words and rearrange their sequence, you can tell completely different stories using the exact same underlying tokens.

```
   [THE PRINTED PAGE OF SENSORY IMPRESSIONS]
   Original Sequential Reading:   [Token 1] -> [Token 2] -> [Token 3] -> [Token 4] -> [Outcome A]
   Reordered Imaginal Reading:    [Token 4] -> [Token 1] -> [Token 3] -> [Token 2] -> [Outcome B]
```

A **dream** is nothing more than uncontrolled fourth-dimensional thinking. In dreams, the sleeping mind rearranges past and future sensory impressions, fusing events separated by years into a single scene. Goddard relates his personal verification:
* *The Dream*: He dreamed he delivered a package to a restaurant; the hostess told him, *"You can't leave that there."* An elevator operator handed him mail, and the night operator waved.
* *The Waking Encounter*: The following day, every single sensory component occurred—but scattered throughout the day across different people and contexts. The deliveryman received the reprimand, the day operator took a tip, the hostess smiled from afar, and the night operator waved hours later.
* *The Deduction*: The sensory impressions were already co-existing in the fourth dimension. The dream had merely fused and rearranged them out of chronological order.

### 5. The Controlled Waking Dream Protocol
Mastery of the fourth dimension requires learning to control the movement of attention in the imaginal space. This is achieved through the **Controlled Waking Dream**:
1. **Attention Minus Effort**: Effort creates physical tension and anchors the mind to the three-dimensional body. Attention must be sustained with absolute zero muscular strain.
2. **Sensory Selection**: Select only those specific sensory tokens which imply that the desire has already been achieved.
3. **The Hypnagogic Vessel**: Enter a state akin to sleep (SATS) where the physical body is immobilized, yet conscious volition remains intact.

## Chapter 3: Power of Imagination

### 1. The Fallacy of Sensory Conformity
Conventional intellect insists that a "true judgment" must conform to the external reality to which it relates. Goddard violently rejects this empiricist dogma as intellectual slavery:
* If an imprisoned man judges his condition solely by his prison walls, he remains trapped by his senses.
* If he boldly assumes he is free, sensory reason calls him self-deceived. Yet if he persists in that assumption, events reorganize to liberate him.
* The ancient spiritual injunction warns never to judge by outward appearances. To imagine lack, illness, or imprisonment for yourself or another is to bear **false witness**, regardless of how objectively real those conditions appear.
* **The Truth of any person is their fulfilled ideal.** Any concept of another short of their liberation is a lie against their divine potential.

### 2. Thinking FROM the End vs. Thinking OF the End
Goddard distinguishes between the amateur visualizer and the fourth-dimensional operator:
* **Thinking OF the End**: Standing on the outside, visualizing the desired state on a mental cinema screen. You remain here, seeing yourself over there. This maintains separation, reinforces distance, and guarantees failure.
* **Thinking FROM the End**: Stepping directly inside the image. You look out through your own eyes, experiencing the scene first-person. You feel the physical textures, hear the voices speaking to you, and anchor the awareness that you are currently situated inside the reality.

### 3. The Sensory Condensation Technique: The Ladder Experiment
To prevent attention from wandering along associative drift (where the mind wanders miles away in seconds), Goddard insists on **drastic condensation**:
* Condense the entire realization of the goal into **one single, simple sensory act** that naturally follows fulfillment (e.g., shaking a hand, putting a key in a lock, climbing a ladder).
* Loop that single act repeatedly in imagination until it takes on the sensory vividness of physical matter.
* In his classic training exercise, Goddard instructed students to immobilize their bodies before sleep, reach out imaginary hands, feel the cold rough wood of a ladder rung, pull themselves up rung by rung, and repeat the climb until falling asleep in the physical sensation of climbing. Invariably, within days, circumstances forced the students to physically climb a ladder in waking life.

### 4. The Awake-in-Dream Anchor Technique
Goddard reveals a formula for waking up directly inside the fourth-dimensional realm:
* Dream images are not flat two-dimensional mental phantoms; they are substantial realities of a dimensionally larger universe.
* If you catch yourself dreaming, immediately reach out and **lay hold of an inanimate, stationary object** within the dream (a solid wooden table, an iron railing, a stone stairway).
* While clutching the object with tactile intensity, command yourself to **AWAKE**.
* You will experience being pulled through yourself, awakening not in your physical bed, but **fully awake and conscious inside the fourth-dimensional world**, holding the object.
* In this awakened 4D state, the lag between assumption and manifestation disappears: *"The external reality instantly mirrors our assumption... the fields are already white to harvest."* (John 4:35).

## Chapter 4: No One to Change But Self

### 1. The Mystical Experience: The Pool of Bethesda Vision
Goddard recounts a transformative mystical experience originally documented in *The Search* (1946) to illustrate that human beings possess no external enemies or separate agents:
* While meditating at sea on the state of absolute purity—where the observer is of too pure eyes to behold iniquity and harbors zero condemnation—he felt himself lifted above physical sensation.
* He became a being of fire dwelling in a body of air, hearing heavenly choruses singing, *"He is risen—He is risen."*
* Walking through the night, he came upon a scene resembling the biblical **Pool of Bethesda**, crowded with hundreds of blind, maimed, halt, and withered folk.
* As he walked past them, he uttered no words and exerted no effort. Yet, simply by virtue of the radiating perfection of his own consciousness, limbs regenerated, blind eyes opened, and the diseased were spontaneously restored to wholeness.
* The mystical lesson was indelible: **The world reflects the state of the observer.** The maimed crowd was not an external problem requiring medical intervention; it was transformed effortlessly the moment consciousness itself was established in absolute perfection.

### 2. The Absolute Law of Non-Interference
To attempt to change the world before altering your concept of self is to wage futile war against the nature of reality.
* Everything done on the physical plane without a prior shift in consciousness is merely **"futile readjustment of surfaces."**
* Those who help or hinder you are simply unconscious servants of the immutable law that shapes outward circumstances in harmony with your inner nature.
* To protest against any external injustice, mistreatment, or disaster is to protest against your own rulership over your destiny.
* If pain or discord exists in your life, you must look within:
> *"The circumstances of my life are too closely related to my conception of myself not to have been formed by my own spirit from some dimensionally larger storehouse of my being."*

### 3. The Transforming Power of Emotional Resonance
Violent emotions precipitate radical spatiotemporal rearrangements. Goddard points to the aftermath of wars: every armed conflict waged for noble ideals is followed by an era of cynicism, materialism, and greed. Why?
* War evokes hatred.
* Hatred forces consciousness to descend to the base level of the conflict.
* By the intensity of hatred, man creates within himself the very character and evils he deplores in his enemy.
* Qualities die purely from **lack of attention**. To eliminate negative states, do not attack them directly; displace them by contemplating whatever is noble, lovely, and of good report.

## Final Mentor Verdict
Neville Goddard's *Out of this World* represents the pinnacle of twentieth-century metaphysical engineering. By grounding mystical intuition in the rigorous spatial mechanics of the fourth dimension, Goddard demystifies prayer, visualization, and destiny. The work provides an uncompromising, empowering mandate: **Stop struggling with shadows in the three-dimensional mirror.** Enter the quiet laboratory of imagination, revise your concept of self, sustain your assumption with effortless fidelity, and watch the physical universe rearrange itself to mirror your inner decree.
