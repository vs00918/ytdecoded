### 1. 623rd RBI Central Board Meeting: Record ₹2.86 Lakh Crore Dividend & CRB at 6.5%
* **Priority:** P1 — Critical / Deep
* **Category:** BANKING_REGULATION
* **Institution:** RBI
* **Event Date:** 2026-05-22
* **Revision Effort:** ~8 min
* **Status:** NOTIFIED

#### What Happened
* The 623rd meeting of the Central Board of Directors of the Reserve Bank of India was held in Mumbai under the chairmanship of **Governor Sanjay Malhotra**.
* Approved a historic surplus transfer (dividend payout) of **₹2,86,588.46 crore** to the Central Government for the accounting year 2025-26, surpassing the previous record of ₹2.68 lakh crore.
* Maintained the **Contingent Risk Buffer (CRB)** at **6.5%** of the RBI balance sheet within the Economic Capital Framework (ECF) target band of 4.5% to 7.5%.

#### Must Memorize
* **Surplus Dividend Transfer:** Approved **₹2,86,588.46 crore** transfer to the Government of India for FY 2025-26 (highest-ever dividend payout by the central bank).
* **Contingent Risk Buffer (CRB) Allocation:** Transferred **₹1,09,379.64 crore** to the CRB (compared to ₹44,861.70 crore in FY25) to maintain the buffer at **6.5%** of the balance sheet.
* **Balance Sheet Expansion:** RBI's balance sheet expanded by **20.61% to ₹91,97,121.08 crore** as of March 31, 2026.
* **Financial Performance:**
  * Gross income increased by **26.42%**.
  * Net income (before risk provisions and statutory fund transfers) reached **₹3,95,972.10 crore**.
* **Economic Capital Framework (ECF) Parameters:** Flexibility to maintain CRB within the range of **4.5% to 7.5%** of total assets.

#### Know / Understand
* Robust earnings from higher interest on domestic and foreign securities, coupled with massive spot forex interventions ($53.13 billion net sales), enabled the central bank to fortify its risk buffers while delivering critical fiscal support to the Union Budget.

#### Exam Focus
* Exact surplus dividend amount transferred to the Government (₹2,86,588.46 crore).
* CRB percentage maintained (6.5%) and ECF prescribed range (4.5%–7.5%).
* Amount transferred to CRB (₹1,09,379.64 crore).
* Total size of the RBI balance sheet as of March 31, 2026 (₹91.97 lakh crore).

### 2. RBI Finalizes Revised Lending & Housing Loan Norms for Urban Co-operative Banks (UCBs)
* **Priority:** P1 — Critical / Deep
* **Category:** BANKING_REGULATION
* **Institution:** RBI
* **Event Date:** 2026-05-15
* **Revision Effort:** ~8 min
* **Status:** NOTIFIED

#### What Happened
* The Reserve Bank of India issued final directions on revised lending norms for Urban Co-operative Banks (UCBs), establishing aggregate and individual caps on unsecured advances and tightening tenure/moratorium limits on housing loans.
* **Effective Date:** Guidelines take effect from **October 1, 2026**.

#### Must Memorize
* **Aggregate & Individual Unsecured Lending Ceilings:**
  * **Aggregate Ceiling:** Unsecured loans and advances capped at **20% of total loans and advances** (shifting from the earlier limit of 10% of total assets).
  * **Tier-Specific Individual Ceilings:**
    * **Tier-1 UCBs:** Maximum **₹5 lakh** per borrower.
    * **Tier-2 UCBs:** Maximum **₹7.5 lakh** per borrower.
    * **Tier-3 & Tier-4 UCBs:** Maximum **₹10 lakh** per borrower.
  * **Priority Sector PSL Exemption:** Unsecured advances up to **₹50,000 per borrower** qualifying as PSL are excluded from the 20% aggregate cap for UCBs meeting RBI's Eligibility Criteria for Business Authorisation (ECBA).
* **Housing Loan Regulations for UCBs:**
  * **Tenure Cap:** Maximum housing loan tenure capped at **20 years** (inclusive of moratorium) for Tier-1 and Tier-2 UCBs. (Tier-3 and Tier-4 UCBs determine tenures under board-approved policies).
  * **Moratorium Restrictions:** Moratorium permitted **only for under-construction properties**, capped at a maximum of **24 months** (moratoriums prohibited for ready-to-move-in housing purchases).

#### Know / Understand
* Restricts unsecured exposure risks in smaller cooperative lenders while ensuring credit flow to micro-borrowers and preventing speculative maturity mismatches in urban housing finance.

#### Exam Focus
* Aggregate unsecured lending cap (20% of total loans).
* Individual unsecured caps across UCB Tiers: Tier-1 (₹5L), Tier-2 (₹7.5L), Tier-3/4 (₹10L).
* Small PSL loan exclusion threshold (₹50,000).
* Housing loan tenure cap for Tier-1/2 UCBs (20 years) and maximum construction moratorium (24 months).
* Effective date: October 1, 2026.

### 3. RBI Overhauls Foreign Exchange Authorization & Forex Correspondent (FxC) Model
* **Priority:** P1 — Critical / Deep
* **Category:** BANKING_REGULATION
* **Institution:** RBI
* **Event Date:** 2026-05-12
* **Revision Effort:** ~8 min
* **Status:** NOTIFIED

#### What Happened
* The Reserve Bank of India overhauled the authorization and renewal framework for entities dealing in foreign exchange under the **Foreign Exchange Management (Authorized Persons) Regulations, 2026**.
* Introduced the **Forex Correspondent (FxC)** model allowing AD Category-I and AD Category-II entities to appoint agents for retail foreign exchange operations.

#### Must Memorize
* **Forex Correspondent (FxC) Operating Framework:**
  * Authorized Dealers (AD-I and AD-II) can appoint FxCs under a principal-agent model.
  * Permitted FxC Operations: (1) Purchase foreign currency notes/coins and travellers' cheques; (2) Sell foreign currency notes/travellers' cheques for foreign travel; (3) Act as MTSS sub-agents.
* **Transaction Limits & Net Worth Requirements:**
  * **AD Category-II Transaction Cap:** Capped at **₹25 lakh per foreign trade transaction**.
  * **Minimum Net Worth:**
    * **AD Category-II:** **₹10 crore**.
    * **AD Category-III:** **₹2 crore**.
  * **Minimum Annual Turnover Requirement:** Must achieve minimum annual turnover of **₹50 crore (AD Category-II)** and **₹10 crore (FFMCs)** within two years of operation and maintain it continuously.
* **Jurisdictional Permissions:** AD Category-I banks retain full access to all current and capital account transactions.

#### Know / Understand
* Expands retail foreign exchange availability and digital remittance points across non-metro regions while enforcing institutional accountability through parent AD bank balance sheets.

#### Exam Focus
* Eligible parent entities to appoint FxCs (AD-I and AD-II).
* AD Category-II transaction ceiling (₹25 lakh per trade transaction).
* Minimum net worth: ₹10 crore (AD-II) and ₹2 crore (AD-III).
* Minimum 2-year turnover thresholds: ₹50 crore (AD-II) and ₹10 crore (FFMC).

### 4. RBI Discontinues Mandatory Investment Fluctuation Reserve (IFR) for Banks
* **Priority:** P1 — Critical / Memorize
* **Category:** BANKING_REGULATION
* **Institution:** RBI
* **Event Date:** 2026-05-18
* **Revision Effort:** ~5 min
* **Status:** NOTIFIED

#### What Happened
* The Reserve Bank of India discontinued the mandatory requirement for commercial banks to create and maintain an **Investment Fluctuation Reserve (IFR)** as an additional buffer against investment portfolio depreciation.
* **Effective Date:** Came into force on **May 18, 2026**.

#### Must Memorize
* **Discontinuation & Balance Transfer Protocol:**
  * Requirement for commercial banks to maintain IFR stands discontinued from May 18, 2026.
  * Existing IFR balances as of May 17, 2026 shall be transferred **'below the line' to Statutory Reserve, General Reserve, or the Balance of Profit and Loss Account**.
* **Foreign Bank Branches in India:**
  * Balance in IFR transferred directly to statutory reserves kept in Indian books or non-repatriable remittable surplus retained in Indian books.
* **Small Finance Banks (SFBs) & Payments Banks (PBs):**
  * Clarified that transfers to IFR must be made from net profit after mandatory statutory appropriations.

#### Know / Understand
* IFR was introduced to absorb yield fluctuations in Available for Sale (AFS) and Held for Trading (HFT) portfolios. With the implementation of revised investment classification norms and mark-to-market accounting under Basel III, standalone IFR had become redundant.

#### Exam Focus
* Effective date of IFR discontinuation: May 18, 2026.
* Accounting treatment of existing IFR balances (transferred 'below the line' to Statutory/General Reserve or P&L).
* Treatment for foreign bank branches.

### 2. **RBI Draft Norms on Specified Non-Financial Assets (SNFAs): Lender Ownership of Collateral** (~3 min)
* **Priority:** P2 — High
* **Category:** BANKING_REGULATION
* **Institution:** RBI
* **Event Date:** 2026-05-14
* **Status:** DRAFT
* **Must Memorize Facts:**
  * **Plain-English Meaning:** Specified Non-Financial Assets (SNFAs) refer to physical collateral (such as industrial property, plant, or real estate) that lenders temporarily take into direct ownership to recover defaulted loans when standard auction/SARFAESI procedures fail.
